Credits

Surface maps

The maps on the planets and moons come from NASA's 3D Resources collection (github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources, folder Images and Textures). NASA content is not subject to copyright in the United States, and NASA asks to be acknowledged as the source (NASA media guidelines). Each map was scaled down and some were edited. NASA does not endorse this page. The credit line and source page of each map, as NASA gives them:

    Drawn by this page

    Mercury, Uranus, the Sun, both ring systems, Earth's clouds, the stars and the asteroid belt are generated by the page's own code and are not photographs. The far side of the Moon is not real, because the source photo shows only the near side. Venus is a radar map, and the Saturn, Neptune and Titan textures are artists' work, as their NASA pages say.

    Figures

    Figures come from NASA's planetary fact sheets and NASA Science pages. A diameter is twice NASA's mean radius, and hours and days are NASA's periods converted from one to the other. Moon counts as read on 15 November 2026: Jupiter 115 (NASA's Jupiter moons page, last updated 21 September 2026), Saturn 274, Uranus 28 and Neptune 16. FIGURES.md, in this chat's files, gives the page behind every figure.
